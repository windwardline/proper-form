The Fit Diagnostic

The difference between looking sloppy and looking sharp isn't the price tag—it's the fit. Focus on these core areas.

Measurement Substitution Pro-Tip

Don't have a new suit to measure yet? No problem. Use an old sports coat, a structured button-down shirt, or even a denim jacket as a baseline. The rules for where a shoulder seam should hit your body remain exactly the same across garments.

1 The Shoulder Seam

The seam connecting the sleeve to the jacket must end exactly at the edge of your shoulder bone. If it extends past, the suit is too large. If it pulls tightly, it's too small.

2 The Trouser Break

The "break" is the fold in the fabric where your pants hit your shoes. For a modern, professional look, aim for a "slight break"—the pant should just gently rest on the top of your shoe laces.

The Golden Rule

"You can buy a $200 suit and spend $50 on tailoring, and it will look infinitely better than a $2,000 suit off the rack."

  • Tailors can easily shorten sleeves.
  • Tailors can easily hem pants.
  • Tailors CANNOT easily fix wide shoulders.

The Seamless Beard Fade

Step 1: The Base

Trim your entire beard to a uniform length using a #3 or #4 guard. This creates an even canvas.

Step 2: The Transition

Drop down one guard size (#2) and trim from the bottom of your neck up to your jawline, flicking outward as you reach the jaw.

Step 3: The Hard Line

Remove the guard completely. Create a sharp, defined line just above your Adam's apple. Shave everything below it clean.
